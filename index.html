Prerequisite Knowledge (Prasyarat Konsep)

Simple Present Tense

Analytical Exposition uses present verbs (*is, are, state, show, think, causes*) to state general truths and opinions.

Opinion vs Fact

Facts provide verifiable data, while opinions state the writer's stance or judgment supported by arguments.

Connectors / Transitions

Words like *firstly, furthermore, on the other hand, consequently* build smooth transitions between ideas.

Analytical Exposition Text: Concept & Structure

An Analytical Exposition Text is a type of argumentative text that evaluates an issue or topic critically to convince readers that the topic is important and deserves serious attention.

Part 1

Thesis (Statement)

Introduces the topic, indicates the writer's position/opinion, and outlines the main points that will be discussed.

Example: "Digital literacy is an essential skill that must be integrated into all high school curricula."
Part 2

Arguments (Body)

Consists of several paragraphs. Each argument starts with a Topic Sentence supported by evidence, research, or logical reasoning.

Example: "Firstly, it prevents students from falling victim to online misinformation and scam attacks."
Part 3

Reiteration (Conclusion)

Restates the writer's thesis and summarizes the main arguments to leave a lasting persuasive impression on readers.

Example: "From the reasons above, it is clear that digital literacy education is crucial for youth."

Interactive Language Features Glossary (Ciri Kebahasaan)

1. Mental Verbs

Verbs that express thinking, feeling, or perceiving.

Examples: feel, believe, realize, understand, think, assume.
2. Causal Conjunctions

Words linking cause and effect relationships.

Examples: because, consequently, as a result, therefore, since.
3. Evaluative Language

Words indicating value judgments or importance.

Examples: crucial, dangerous, beneficial, vital, significant.

Why Digital Literacy is Essential for High School Students

In the modern information era, digital literacy is no longer a luxury but an absolute necessity. High school students are constantly exposed to vast amounts of online information, making critical evaluation skills indispensable.

Firstly, digital literacy empowers students to distinguish between credible scientific research and harmful fake news. Without proper training, young individuals can easily fall prey to scams or spreading misinformation. Secondly, mastering digital tools enhances future career readiness, as nearly all modern industries require digital communication and collaboration software skills.

Therefore, integrating comprehensive digital literacy training into the school curriculum is crucial for developing responsible and capable future citizens.
